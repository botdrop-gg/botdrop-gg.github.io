1. Free: run it on your own PC ($0)

This costs nothing. Our one-click launcher runs the bot on your Windows PC. You don't need Python, a hosting account or a credit card. It takes about 5 minutes the first time. The only limit: the bot is online only while your PC is on and awake.

You need: a Windows 10 or 11 PC, your Discord account, and a Discord server you own or manage.

  1. Download the launcher. Open your Gumroad library and download BotDrop-<Product>-windows.exe (for example BotDrop-Kit-windows.exe). It's a separate file next to the zip. Bought the bundle? Each bot has its own launcher in the bundle’s file list; download the .exe for the bot you want.
    Make a folder for it, for example Documents\BotDrop, and move the .exe there. The launcher saves its settings next to itself, so keep it in that folder.
  2. Open it, and get past the blue Windows box. Double-click the .exe. Windows will probably show a blue box that says "Windows protected your PC".

    Why it appears: this box is Windows SmartScreen. It shows up for new programs that are not code-signed (a paid digital signature that tells Windows who made a file) and that few people have downloaded yet. Our launchers are not code-signed yet, so you will likely see it. It does not mean Windows found a virus. Some antivirus apps may warn about new unsigned programs for the same reason.

    What to do, in order:

    1. Check the file first (about 1 minute). Every file has a fingerprint called a SHA-256 checksum. If even one byte changed, the fingerprint changes. In the folder with the .exe, click the address bar, type powershell and press Enter. Then type Get-FileHash .\BotDrop-Kit-windows.exe (use your file's name) and press Enter. Compare the long code with the one on our checksums page. Capital or small letters don't matter. If it doesn't match, don't run it: download it again from your Gumroad library, and email hello@getbotdrop.com if it still doesn't match.
    2. In the blue box, click More info.
    3. Click Run anyway.

    Only do this for a file you downloaded from your Gumroad library (or, for the free Free Games Radar, from our Kit confirmation email) whose checksum matches.

  3. Follow the setup window. It opens with a Welcome page and walks you through every click, with pictures. Keep it open next to your browser and click Next after each step. The Full guide button opens this page.

    What the setup asks depends on your bot:

    • Bots that reply to commands (Gaming Community Kit, Mod Bot) need a bot token. A token is like a password that lets your bot log in to Discord. Follow steps A to D below.
    • Alert bots (Deal Alert, Free Games Radar, Patch Tracker, Player Count, What To Play, Stream Alert, Intel) only need a webhook link. Follow step E.
    • Web tools (Review Verdict, Repo Health) need nothing from Discord. They simply open in your web browser.

A. Create your bot and copy its token

  1. Click Open Discord Developer Portal in the launcher (or go to discord.com/developers/applications) and log in with your Discord account.
  2. Click New Application (top right), type your bot's name, tick the box to agree, and click Create.
  3. Click Bot in the left menu. Here you can set your bot's picture and name.
  4. Scroll to Privileged Gateway Intents, switch on Server Members Intent and Message Content Intent, and click Save Changes.
  5. Scroll back up and click Reset Token, then Yes, do it!, then Copy. Discord only shows it once. If you lose it, just reset it again. Never share it.
  6. Paste the token into the launcher's box (Ctrl+V) and click Next. The launcher checks it with Discord and tells you in plain words if it's wrong.

B. Invite the bot to your server

Click the big Open invite link button in the launcher. The link already has the right permissions. Pick your server, then click Continue → Authorize. The launcher waits until the bot has really joined, and only then lets you click Next.

If the button doesn't work, use Discord's own link: click Installation in the left menu, tick Guild Install, add the scopes bot and applications.commands and the permissions shown in the launcher (Gaming Community Kit: View Channels, Send Messages, Embed Links, Read Message History, Manage Messages, Moderate Members, Manage Channels, Manage Roles, View Audit Log and Manage Webhooks), click Save Changes, then copy the Discord Provided Link and open it.

Still stuck? Build the link yourself: open General Information in the Developer Portal, copy the Application ID, and open https://discord.com/oauth2/authorize?client_id=YOUR_APPLICATION_ID&scope=bot+applications.commands&permissions=1100317027472 with your ID in place of YOUR_APPLICATION_ID. That number is the Kit’s permission list above (the same link the launcher makes).

C. Your Server ID (optional, but recommended)

It makes slash commands like /rules appear right away instead of after up to an hour. In Discord, open User Settings (the ⚙️ next to your name) → Advanced, and switch on Developer Mode. Then right-click your server's icon on the left and click Copy Server ID. Paste it into the launcher. You can leave it empty.

D. Click Start: the status window and tray icon

On the last page, click Start (in the Gaming Community Kit: Start the bot). A small status window shows Online in: <your server>, or a warning if the bot isn't in a server yet. Your bot now shows as online in Discord. Try /rules.

Gaming Community Kit: run /setup next. In any channel, type /setup (server admins only). The bot creates #free-games, #deals, #patch-notes, #mod-log (hidden from members), #welcome and a Verified role. Channels that already exist are reused, so it’s safe to run again. Then click Open dashboard in the status window to switch features on or off and change settings. The dashboard opens in your browser at 127.0.0.1 and only works on this computer.

When do the first posts appear? A few seconds after /setup. Once the channels exist, the bot checks free games, Steam deals and patch notes straight away and posts anything new. After that it checks on a timer: free games and deals every 6 hours, patch notes every 2 hours. A channel that stays empty just means there’s nothing new right now (no free game, no deal at your target price, or no recent patch for your games). Nothing is posted twice, even if you run /setup again or restart the bot.

Anti-nuke watches for its first 7 days (BETA). Auto-moderation works from the start. On a new install, anti-nuke (once you turn it on) only posts in #mod-log which roles it would have removed and doesn’t remove them, so you can check its limits first. If your host has no saved disk, that week starts again on each deploy; set ENFORCEMENT_MODE=enforce to skip it. When you’re happy, click Turn on enforcement at the top of the dashboard, or type /setup enforcement:True. Updating from Kit 2.0? Your bot keeps enforcing as before.

You can close the status window: the bot keeps running from the BotDrop icon in the system tray, at the bottom right near the clock. If you don't see it, click the ^ arrow. Right-click the icon for its menu:

  • Status: opens the status window again.
  • Open dashboard (Gaming Community Kit): opens the local dashboard in your browser.
  • Open log: shows what the bot is doing (useful if something goes wrong).
  • Start with Windows: tick it and the bot starts by itself every time you log in to Windows. Click it again to turn it off.
  • Stop bot: turns the bot off.

E. Alert bots: create a webhook

  1. In Discord, open your server's menu (click the server name at the top left) → Server Settings → Integrations → Webhooks → New Webhook.
  2. Click the new webhook, choose the channel where alerts should appear, then click Copy Webhook URL.
  3. Paste it into the launcher and click Send test message. A message should appear in that channel. Then click Next and Start. The status window and tray icon work as in step D.

The one limit: it runs only while your PC is on. If the PC sleeps, shuts down or loses internet, the bot goes offline until the PC is back (with Start with Windows ticked, it comes back by itself when you log in). To stop your PC from sleeping, open Settings → System → Power and set sleep to Never while plugged in. Need it online all the time? See Run it 24/7 below.

Good to know: your token or webhook is saved only on your PC, in a file called .env next to the launcher. Delete .env to run the setup again. To change games or channels, open config.yaml in the same folder with Notepad (alert bots also have a Settings button in the status window), save it, then stop and start the bot again. The Gaming Community Kit also has the local dashboard (Open dashboard) for most settings, with no restart needed.

Linux (short version, beta)

  1. Download BotDrop-<Product>-linux from your Gumroad library (bundle: BotDrop-launchers-linux.zip) into its own folder.
  2. Make it runnable: right-click → Properties → Allow executing as program, or run chmod +x BotDrop-*-linux.
  3. Double-click it (or run ./BotDrop-Kit-linux) and follow the same setup window as above.
  4. There's no tray icon on Linux: keep the status window open, because closing it asks to stop the bot. To start it when you log in, add it to your desktop's Startup Applications.

Mac: there's no launcher yet. Use the Python steps in your bot's README.md.

Before you start, have your bot token (and Server ID) or webhook link ready, from section 1. If your bot already runs on your PC, stop it there first, because the same token shouldn't run in two places.

Steps 1–3 (both hosts): put your bot's files on GitHub

  1. Unzip your bot and make a free GitHub account at github.com/signup. Unzip the file from Gumroad. Inside is one folder, for example 09-discord-gaming-community-kit. GitHub is where your bot's files will live, so the host can find them.
  2. Create a private repository (a "repository" is just a folder on GitHub). Go to github.com/new. Name it, for example my-discord-bot. The visibility choice starts on Public, so change it to Private. Then click Create repository.
    Keep it Private. Your license doesn't allow sharing the template publicly.
  3. Upload your bot's files. On the new repository page, click uploading an existing file. Open the unzipped folder on your computer, select everything inside it (not the folder itself), and drag it onto the page. Then click Commit changes.
    Check it worked: you should see Dockerfile, render.yaml and railway.json in the main list, not inside another folder. To change the bot's settings, click config.yaml, then the ✏️ pencil icon, edit it, and click Commit changes.

2a. Render: about $7.25/month ✅ tested by us

✅ Tested Oct 16, 2026: we followed these exact steps with a new GitHub and Render account and the Gaming Community Kit. The bot went live and answered /rules in Discord. Websites change their buttons from time to time, so if something looks different, email hello@getbotdrop.com.

  1. Make a Render account at render.com. Signing up with GitHub is easiest.
  2. Create the bot on Render. In the Render dashboard, click + New, then Blueprint.
    1. Render first shows an Add Card window. Enter your card. Render places a temporary $1 hold to check the card. It is refunded.
    2. Render asks to install its app on GitHub. Choose Only select repositories, pick your bot's repository, and confirm. GitHub may ask you to Confirm access. If it does, choose Verify via email and type in the code GitHub emails you.
    3. Back on Render, your repository appears in the list. Click Connect next to it.
  3. Paste your secrets. You'll see a form with a Blueprint Name (any name is fine) and Branch (leave it on main). Render asks for the settings your bot needs, like DISCORD_TOKEN and GUILD_ID, or DISCORD_WEBHOOK_URL. These are called environment variables: settings stored on Render instead of inside your files. Paste each value. Leave optional ones (like AI keys) blank if you don't use them.
  4. Click Deploy Blueprint. The form lists something like "Create background worker … (Starter)". Starter is the smallest always-on server, $7 a month, plus $0.25 for the 1 GB disk. In our test it was live within a minute.
  5. Check it's alive. Open your new service and click Logs. Look for Your service is live. Command bots then print Logged in as followed by your bot's name, and alert bots print what they checked. Your bot now shows as online in Discord. Try /rules.

Changing settings later: edit config.yaml on GitHub. Render notices and restarts the bot by itself. To change a token or webhook, open the service in Render and click Environment.

Web tools (Review Verdict, Repo Health): the same steps create a free Render website instead ($0). It sleeps after 15 minutes without visitors and takes about a minute to wake up.

2b. Railway: about $5/month ⚠️ not yet tested by us

⚠️ Not yet tested by us. These steps follow Railway's official help pages (checked Oct 16, 2026) and the railway.json file in your zip, but we haven't clicked through them with a new account yet. If something doesn't match, email hello@getbotdrop.com. Render (above) is the option we've tested.

  1. Make a Railway account at railway.com. Signing up with GitHub is easiest. New accounts start on a free trial with $5 of credit for 30 days. To keep the bot running after that, choose the Hobby plan ($5/month) in your account's billing settings and add a card. (Railway's Free plan only gives $1 a month, which won't keep a bot online all month.)
  2. Create the project. In the dashboard, click New Project, then GitHub repo (Deploy from GitHub repo). If asked, let Railway access GitHub and choose Only select repositories with your bot's repository. Click your repository, then choose Add variables (not Deploy Now).
  3. Paste your secrets. In your service's Variables tab, click New Variable for each setting from your bot's README: DISCORD_TOKEN and GUILD_ID for command bots, or DISCORD_WEBHOOK_URL for alert bots. Also add RAILWAY_RUN_UID with the value 0. Our bots need it to save to the disk in the next step.
  4. Add a 1 GB disk so the bot remembers what it already posted. Right-click an empty part of the project canvas, choose Volume, pick your bot's service, and set the mount path to /data. It costs about $0.15 a month, inside the included $5.
  5. Click Deploy at the top of the canvas to apply your changes. Railway builds your bot from the Dockerfile.
  6. Check it's alive. Click your service, open Deployments, and click View logs. Command bots print Logged in as followed by your bot's name, and alert bots print what they checked. Try /rules in Discord.

Changing settings later: edit config.yaml on GitHub, and Railway redeploys by itself. To change a token or webhook, edit it in the Variables tab. To keep costs predictable, you can set a spending limit in Railway's usage settings.

Web tools (Review Verdict, Repo Health): after the deploy, open the service's Settings → Networking and click Generate Domain to get a web address. No disk is needed.

Common problems

Windows says "Windows protected your PC"

This appears because the launcher is new and not code-signed yet. First check that the file's SHA-256 checksum matches our checksums page (how), then click More info, then Run anyway.

"Improper token" or "That token didn't work"

The token is wrong or was reset. Click Reset Token in the Discord Developer Portal and copy the new one. On your PC, delete .env next to the launcher and run it again. On Render, paste it under Environment; on Railway, under Variables.

The bot is online but ignores commands or doesn't see messages

Switch on Server Members Intent and Message Content Intent on the Bot page, and make sure you invited it with applications.commands. Without a Server ID, slash commands can take up to an hour to appear. Check that GUILD_ID is your server's ID.

The bot went offline when my PC was off

That's expected with the free PC option. Tick Start with Windows so it comes back when you log in, turn off sleep, or move it to 24/7 hosting.

The host can't find render.yaml, railway.json or a Dockerfile

You probably uploaded the folder instead of what's inside it. On GitHub, your files should be at the top level. Delete the repository and upload again, selecting the files inside the folder.

Render asks for a card, or says workers aren't free

That's expected. Always-on bots need Render's smallest paid server, $7 a month.

Alerts don't arrive

Check the webhook link is pasted in full (it starts with https://discord.com/api/webhooks/) and that the webhook still exists in Server Settings → Integrations. Alert bots check on a timer (for example every 6 hours for Free Games Radar), so the first post can take a while. The first run may only record what already exists, so you don't get flooded with old posts.

The bot posts old alerts again after a restart

On a host, keep the 1 GB disk mounted at /data. It's where the bot remembers what it already posted. On Railway, also check RAILWAY_RUN_UID is 0.
